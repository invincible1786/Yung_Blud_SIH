Research Summary & Algorithmic Innovations

This research suite investigates the performance of Quantum-behaved Particle Swarm Optimization (QPSO) applied to the Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP) constrained on real OpenStreetMap road networks in Kharagpur, West Bengal.

Why Baseline QPSO Failed Initially

Standard continuous swarm decoders assign customer priority keys and split routes using a greedy delimiter: when vehicle capacity $Q$ is exceeded, the current route is terminated and a new truck is dispatched. This naive split frequently strands stops in under-filled vehicles, driving vehicle counts from 10 up to 18 trucks and triggering catastrophic penalty costs.

How QPSO-Optimized Solved It

  • Prins Split Algorithm: Replaced the greedy split with an optimal polynomial dynamic programming search over an auxiliary Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG), guaranteeing minimum possible travel distance for any customer permutation.
  • FFD Bin-Packing Seeding: Proved that vehicle fleet counts must respect the bin-packing lower bound $K_{min} = \lceil \sum d_i / Q \rceil$. Injected an FFD seed particle to ensure the swarm explores the true minimum vehicle count.
  • Inter-Route 2-opt*: Evaluated cross-route edge swaps $(u, u_{next})$ and $(v, v_{next})$, allowing routes to uncross and balance stop assignments, breaking the post-seeding distance plateau.
